# Chapter 10. Protect Your Heart and Kidneys

## Why this chapter matters most

Heart and blood vessel disease is the main cause of death in people with type 2 diabetes. Kidney disease is one of the most common complications. Both are largely preventable, and the tools are well proven: blood pressure control, cholesterol lowering, not smoking, and medicines that protect the heart and kidneys directly.

## Blood pressure

**Target:** below 130/80 mmHg for most people with diabetes, if it can be reached safely.

### Measuring at home

Home blood pressure readings are often more accurate than readings at the doctor’s office.

1.  **Use an automatic upper-arm monitor** that has been clinically validated. Wrist monitors are less reliable.
2.  **Use the right cuff size.** A cuff that is too small gives falsely high readings.
3.  **Sit quietly for 5 minutes** with your back supported, feet flat on the floor, and arm resting at heart level.
4.  **Do not smoke, drink coffee or exercise** for 30 minutes before.
5.  **Take two readings, one minute apart,** in the morning before medicines and in the evening.
6.  **Do this for 7 days** before a doctor’s visit and bring the average.

### Lowering blood pressure without medicine

- **Lose weight:** about 1 mmHg lower for each kg lost.
- **Eat less salt:** below 5 g a day can lower systolic pressure by 4–5 mmHg.
- **Eat more potassium-rich foods:** vegetables, fruit, legumes and nuts, unless you have advanced kidney disease or take medicines that raise potassium.
- **Exercise regularly:** both aerobic and strength training lower blood pressure.
- **Limit alcohol.**
- **Sleep well,** and treat sleep apnoea.

### Medicines

Most people with diabetes and high blood pressure need medicine, often two or more. **ACE inhibitors** (such as ramipril, perindopril) or **angiotensin receptor blockers** (such as losartan, valsartan, telmisartan) are preferred, especially if there is protein in the urine, because they also protect the kidneys. Other common options are **calcium channel blockers** (such as amlodipine) and **thiazide-like diuretics** (such as indapamide). Combination tablets reduce the number of pills.

## Cholesterol

LDL cholesterol is a direct cause of the fatty plaques that block arteries. The lower your LDL, the lower your risk, and there is no known level that is too low for heart protection.

**Targets** (European Society of Cardiology, 2025 update, unchanged from 2019):

- **High risk:** LDL below 1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) and at least 50% lower than your untreated level.
- **Very high risk** (organ damage, several risk factors, or established heart disease): LDL below 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) and at least 50% lower than untreated.

### Lifestyle

Diet changes can lower LDL by 10–20%:

- Replace butter, lard and fatty meats with olive oil, nuts and fish.
- Eat more soluble fibre: oats, barley, beans, lentils, apples and citrus fruit.
- Avoid trans fats.
- Lose weight if needed.

### Medicines

- **Statins** (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin) are the first choice. They lower LDL by 30–50% or more and cut the risk of heart attack and stroke by about a quarter for every 1 mmol/L reduction in LDL. Muscle aches are less common than many people think; if they happen, a different statin or a lower dose often works.
- **Ezetimibe** is added if the target is not reached.
- **PCSK9 inhibitors, inclisiran or bempedoic acid** are options for people who still do not reach target or cannot take statins.

Statins raise blood sugar very slightly, but their heart protection far outweighs this.

**Triglycerides** fall with weight loss, less alcohol, fewer refined carbohydrates and sugars, and better blood sugar control.

## Kidneys

Diabetic kidney disease develops silently for years. The first sign is albumin leaking into the urine. Later, kidney function (eGFR) falls. Without treatment, it can lead to dialysis. With modern treatment, progression can be slowed dramatically.

### How to protect your kidneys

1.  **Get tested every year:** eGFR (blood) and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (urine).
2.  **Control blood pressure,** ideally with an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker if albumin is raised.
3.  **Take an SGLT2 inhibitor** if you have kidney disease. Large trials, such as DAPA-CKD with dapagliflozin, showed slower decline in kidney function and fewer deaths. If you already take Xigduo, you are taking one.
4.  **Ask about finerenone** if you have persistent albumin in the urine despite treatment.
5.  **Ask about a GLP-1 receptor agonist.** Semaglutide has been shown to slow kidney disease progression in people with type 2 diabetes.
6.  **Control blood sugar.**
7.  **Avoid kidney-harming medicines:** regular use of ibuprofen, diclofenac and other NSAIDs can damage kidneys. Use paracetamol for occasional pain, and ask your pharmacist.
8.  **Stay hydrated,** and follow your sick-day rules.
9.  **Do not smoke.**

## Heart failure

Heart failure, in which the heart pumps less effectively, is common in diabetes and often missed. Warning signs are breathlessness on exertion or lying flat, swollen ankles, rapid weight gain of 2 kg or more in a few days, and tiredness. SGLT2 inhibitors reduce hospital admissions for heart failure. Report any of these symptoms promptly.

## Aspirin

- **If you have had a heart attack, stroke or artery disease,** low-dose aspirin is usually recommended.
- **If you have not,** aspirin is not routinely recommended for people with diabetes, because the risk of bleeding often equals or outweighs the benefit. Do not start aspirin on your own.

> **What to do**
>
> - Measure your blood pressure at home for a week before each doctor’s visit. Aim for below 130/80.
> - Ask your doctor about your LDL target and whether you need a statin, or a stronger one.
> - Have your kidney blood and urine tests every year.
> - Avoid regular use of ibuprofen and similar painkillers.
> - Report breathlessness, swollen ankles or sudden weight gain promptly.
