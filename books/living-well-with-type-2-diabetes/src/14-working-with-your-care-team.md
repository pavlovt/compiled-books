# Chapter 13. Working with Your Care Team

## You are the most important member

You spend a few hours a year with health professionals and the rest of the year managing diabetes yourself. The best outcomes come when you see yourself as the manager of your own care, with professionals as expert advisers.

Your team may include:

- **Your general practitioner (GP),** who coordinates care, prescribes medicines and arranges tests.
- **An endocrinologist,** a specialist in diabetes and hormones. In Bulgaria, people with diabetes are usually registered with an endocrinologist for regular follow-up and for prescriptions of reimbursed medicines.
- **An eye doctor (ophthalmologist).**
- **A podiatrist or foot specialist,** if you have foot problems.
- **A dentist.**
- **A dietitian.**
- **A cardiologist or nephrologist,** if you have heart or kidney disease.
- **A pharmacist,** who can check interactions and explain medicines.
- **A diabetes education programme,** if available. Structured education improves HbA1c and confidence.

## Preparing for appointments

Appointments are often short. Preparation makes them count.

**Before the visit:**

- Write down your questions, most important first.
- Bring your medicine list, including supplements.
- Bring your home blood sugar and blood pressure records, or your CGM report.
- Note any symptoms, side effects or problems since the last visit.
- Bring your numbers table (Chapter 2).

**During the visit:**

- Ask for your test results and write them down.
- Ask what your targets are and whether you are reaching them.
- If a change is proposed, ask why, what side effects to watch for, and when it will be reviewed.
- If you do not understand, ask again. It is your right.

**Useful questions to ask:**

1.  What are my HbA1c, blood pressure and LDL targets, and am I meeting them?
2.  Do my medicines protect my heart and kidneys? Would an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 medicine help me?
3.  What should I do on sick days? Which medicines should I pause?
4.  When was my last eye check, urine albumin test and foot examination?
5.  Do I need a statin? What is my cardiovascular risk?
6.  Am I a candidate for diabetes remission through weight loss?
7.  Which vaccines do I need?
8.  Should my vitamin B12 be checked?

## The annual care checklist

Use this list to make sure nothing is missed each year.

## Carrying information with you

Keep a card in your wallet or a note on your phone’s emergency screen with:

- “I have type 2 diabetes.”
- Your medicines and doses.
- Allergies.
- An emergency contact.
- Your doctor’s name and phone number.

> **What to do**
>
> - Prepare a short list of questions before every appointment.
> - Bring your medicine list and home readings.
> - Use the annual checklist, and ask for any missing tests.
> - Ask whether a structured diabetes education programme is available to you.
