# How to Use This Book

This book is a practical guide for living well with type 2 diabetes. It is written for one reader in particular: a working adult who already takes diabetes medicine, wants to understand the condition properly, and wants a clear list of what to do.

It is not a replacement for your doctor. Your doctor knows your test results, your other conditions and your full list of medicines. This book helps you ask better questions, make better daily choices, and notice problems early. Where the book and your doctor disagree about your own treatment, follow your doctor and ask them to explain the difference.

The book is arranged from the most important habits to the more specialised topics:

- **Chapters 1–2** explain what type 2 diabetes is and which numbers to track.
- **Chapters 3–7** cover the daily habits that change the course of the disease: weight, food, movement, sleep and stress.
- **Chapters 8–9** cover medicines, blood sugar monitoring and emergencies.
- **Chapters 10–11** cover protecting your heart, kidneys, eyes, feet and other organs.
- **Chapters 12–13** cover supplements, myths and working with your care team.
- **Chapter 14** turns everything into a 12-week action plan.
- **The appendices** hold checklists, a glossary and sources.

Each chapter ends with a short box called **“What to do”**. If you read nothing else, read those boxes.

A note on units. Blood sugar is measured in **mmol/L** in Bulgaria and most of Europe, and in **mg/dL** in the United States and in many online sources. This book gives both. To convert, divide mg/dL by 18 to get mmol/L. For example, 180 mg/dL is 10.0 mmol/L. HbA1c is given as a percentage and in mmol/mol; for example, 7% is 53 mmol/mol.
